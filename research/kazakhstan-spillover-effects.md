## Main conclusions

### France: limited spillover is the working assessment; oil receipts are the main downside channel

The user's initial assessment was that developments in France have a low probability of a negative spillover to Kazakhstan, with lower oil export revenues as a possible route.

The working analysis is that a shock confined to France should have a smaller impact than one spreading across Europe or global financial markets. Fiscal adjustment or uncertainty could weaken French activity; broader European weakness could reduce oil demand and prices; lower realised oil prices would reduce Kazakhstan's export receipts.

This assessment requires verification of direct trade exposure and broader oil-market effects. France's share of Kazakhstan exports does not itself measure global oil-price sensitivity. A loss of one buyer might be partly offset by other buyers, subject to transport, contracts and product specifications.

### United States: rising Treasury yields can affect financing and import costs

The user clarified that the US situation of interest is **rising yields**, rather than tariffs.

Higher US Treasury yields can raise external financing costs through the benchmark rate even with unchanged credit spreads. They can also encourage flows toward USD assets and put pressure on the tenge. If the tenge weakens against import invoice currencies, local-currency import costs can rise even with unchanged foreign-currency prices.

The import-price channel is conditional. Higher Treasury yields do not mechanically imply a stronger dollar, a weaker tenge or higher Kazakhstan consumer inflation. Oil prices, monetary policy, FX buffers, capital flows and global growth can offset or amplify the effect.

## France's fiscal background: questions and framework

Persistent spending–revenue imbalances accumulate public debt. Higher borrowing rates feed into debt-service costs as debt is refinanced. Growth and political constraints can complicate fiscal consolidation.

This is a framework to investigate, not a verified explanation of a specific recent budget episode. The requested background should establish:
- Where the budget problem originated, including revenue shortfalls, spending pressures and policy decisions.
- General-government deficit as a percentage of GDP.
- General-government debt as a percentage of GDP.
- Interest spending and budget targets.
- Which figures are actual outcomes, forecasts or government targets.

Suggested chart window: 2019 through the latest available year. Show deficit and debt in separate charts to distinguish annual flows from accumulated stocks.

## US yield background: distinguish the drivers

Potential drivers include:
- Changes in expected short-term interest rates due to inflation or stronger activity.
- A higher term premium demanded for holding long-maturity debt.
- Fiscal deficits and debt issuance, interacting with investor demand and monetary conditions.

A yield rise driven by stronger growth may have different spillovers from one driven by inflation or fiscal risk. Fix the episode dates before assigning causes. Term-premium estimates are model-based.

## Illustrative data: oil export receipts

Assumptions: unchanged export volume and product mix; USD receipts; baseline index = 100. No attribution to France and no fiscal-revenue forecast.

| Oil price change | Export receipts index | Receipts change |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0% | 100 | 0% |
| −5% | 95 | −5% |
| −10% | 90 | −10% |
| −20% | 80 | −20% |

Receipt ratio = (1 + price change) × (1 + volume change).

For example, a 10% price fall with 5% volume growth produces an index of 94.5, a 5.5% receipts decline. Actual tax receipts need not move one-for-one with gross oil exports.

## Illustrative data: exchange rates and import costs

Assumptions: unchanged USD import prices; baseline KZT cost index = 100. No CPI forecast.

| Increase in KZT per USD | KZT import cost index | Cost increase |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0% | 100 | 0% |
| 5% | 105 | 5% |
| 10% | 110 | 10% |
| 20% | 120 | 20% |

Local-currency import cost = foreign-currency price × KZT per unit of that currency.

A 10% rise in KZT per USD is not identical to a 10% fall in the reciprocal USD value of the tenge; the latter is approximately 9.09%. Other invoice currencies may behave differently. Retail inflation depends on import content, margins, contracts, timing and monetary policy.
