# Verified economic evidence: Kazakhstan spillovers
Verified on 9 October 2026. Scope: 2025 annual background and latest available observations through October 2026. Observation dates differ because publication lags differ. This is a verified core evidence set, not a completed causal study. It supplements the historical discussion record in kazakhstan-spillover-effects.md.

## France: deficit improved, but debt and interest costs rose

Source: [INSEE, 29 May 2026, tables 1 and 4](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/8997691).

| Year | Deficit, % GDP (positive magnitude) | Gross Maastricht debt, % GDP |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 2.4 | 97.9 |
| 2022 | 4.7 | 111.4 |
| 2023 | 5.4 | 109.5 |
| 2024 | 5.8 | 112.6 |
| 2025 | 5.1 | 115.7 |

2025 deficit: EUR 152.5bn. Spending: 57.3% of GDP; revenue: 52.2%. Nominal revenue grew 3.9%; spending grew 2.5%. Interest expenditure excluding the FISIM correction reached EUR 64.7bn, up 11.2%.

Revision rule: use 115.7% for 2025 debt and 112.6% for 2024 from this release. Do not mix these with preliminary 115.6% or an older 113.0% estimate.

### Where the pressure came from

[French Treasury, 21 September 2026](https://www.tresor.economie.gouv.fr/Articles/2026/09/21/finances-publiques-une-situation-degradee-un-redressement-necessaire) identifies successive crisis-support measures alongside debt service, defence and ageing pressures.

[Trésor-Éco 356, January 2025, pp. 1 and 7–8](https://www.tresor.economie.gouv.fr/Articles/e07b5a5a-8084-4b40-aee5-fdb4a128904c/files/92ef2169-a48c-457d-9725-570fd4ac6d72) documents weaker tax receipts and nominal growth, lagged corporate/income-tax effects and local spending pressures. Its EUR 40.1bn downward revision concerns **2024 tax-revenue forecasts between budget proposals**, not a final audited revenue loss. Its deficit forecasts were subsequently superseded.

| Indicator | 2025 value |
|---|---:|
| Goods exports | USD 79.0412bn |
| Goods imports | USD 64.847bn |
| Export growth, nominal USD | −3.2% |
| Import growth, nominal USD | +7.4% |

| Russia, share of imports | 29.7% |
| China, share of imports | 29.2% |
| US, share of imports | 3.3% |
| France, share of imports | 2.4% |

These are goods, not goods and services. Nominal import growth is not an import-price index.

Source: [World Bank WITS / UN Comtrade, Kazakhstan-reported exports, HS 2709, 2025](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/KAZ/year/2025/tradeflow/Exports/partner/ALL/product/2709).

| Crude-export measure | Value |
|---|---:|
| World value | USD 39.9772181bn |
| World quantity | 76.2943 million tonnes |
| France value | USD 3.00958808bn |
| France quantity | 5.56949 million tonnes |
| France share of crude export value, calculated | 7.53% |

WITS displays values in thousands of USD and quantities in kg; units above are converted. France's crude purchases equal 3.81% of total goods export value when divided by the BNS total. This is **not** France's total share of all exports.

Cross-check: WITS crude value / BNS goods exports = 50.58%, close to BNS's rounded 50.5%; coverage/revisions can differ. France-reported mirror imports differ from Kazakhstan-reported exports; use reporter-side exports consistently for exposure.

Illustrative sensitivity anchored to actual 2025 crude receipts: a 10% realised-price fall at unchanged volumes would reduce receipts by approximately USD 4.00bn. This remains a scenario, not a forecast or a France-attributable loss.

## Kazakhstan: latest FX and prices qualify the risk narrative

[NBK FX bulletin, 1 October 2026](https://nationalbank.kz/en/news/informacionnye-soobshcheniya/20378): end-September KZT/USD was **440.49**. NBK reports **4.6% tenge appreciation** during September and no FX interventions. Use NBK's stated appreciation convention; do not silently equate it with the reciprocal exchange-rate percentage.

[BNS September 2026 CPI](https://stat.gov.kz/en/industries/economv/prices/publications/517844/): annual inflation **9.3%**, down from **9.8%** in August; monthly inflation **0.6%**. Non-food inflation **11.0%** year-on-year. CPI and non-food CPI are not import-price indices.

Historical context: [NBK, 23 January 2026](https://nationalbank.kz/en/news/press-relizy/18296) reports end-2025 inflation of **12.3%** and non-food inflation of **11.1%**, with exchange-rate appreciation contributing to non-food disinflation. This is NBK's assessment, not an estimated causal coefficient.

## Conclusions the evidence supports

- **France:** a meaningful crude buyer, but Kazakhstan's larger vulnerability is its aggregate oil-price exposure. Limited spillover remains a qualitative assessment; these figures do not establish a low numerical probability or exclude financial channels.
- **US:** rising Treasury yields are confirmed. Financing-cost and FX channels remain plausible, but the latest Kazakhstan observations show appreciation and disinflation rather than realised depreciation-driven inflation.
- **Import costs:** retain a conditional headline: “Higher US yields could raise import costs if they weaken the tenge.” Do not state that the data prove US yields have already caused Kazakhstan inflation.
- **No causal attribution:** the series have different frequencies and do not isolate France or US effects from oil, domestic policy and other shocks.
